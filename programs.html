Our programs

What we actually do

Four named programs, each built around a real gap: the patient nobody visits, the person who falls back into crisis after treatment, the family quietly running out.

Where we are today: The Balm was registered in November 2025 and our first outreach is being planned. This page describes how each program works. As we deliver them, we’ll add real dates, places and results on our stories page — never made-up numbers.

Hospital visits
Four times a year

Reach the Sick

Because no one should recover alone.

Four times a year, our team visits people admitted in hospital — those facing long treatment, few visitors, or a ward far from home.

We help with costs where we can. But the heart of this program is being there: sitting with patients, listening, and easing the fear and loneliness that treatment alone doesn’t touch.

Where a patient agrees, we keep in touch after the visit instead of leaving encouragement at the bedside.

Crisis to independence
Full-cycle program

OPTRTE

Operation Pick, Treat, Rehabilitate, Teach & Empower.

Our most intensive program. Instead of offering relief and moving on, OPTRTE carries one person all the way from crisis to earning a living.

People are chosen at random, not by application — so help reaches people who would never think to ask, and who most programs miss.

Pick

We identify and take in an individual in urgent need — chosen at random so the overlooked are not passed over again.

Treat

We provide the medical care their condition requires, start to finish.

Rehabilitate

We support their physical and psychosocial recovery, so they leave stronger, not just discharged.

Teach

We equip them with a skill or trade they can earn from.

Empower

We set them up with the tools or starter support to sustain themselves — then we step back.

Family support
Ongoing

The Balm Basket

For households under quiet strain.

Not every need looks like a medical emergency. Sometimes it’s food on the table, a broken home, or simply getting through the month.

We give direct financial support alongside food, provisions and household essentials — delivered quietly and with dignity. The point is that a family is steadier afterwards, not that the giving was seen.

Annual review
Once a year · By invitation

The Balm Summit

Where we hold ourselves to account.

At the close of each year, our Board of Trustees and invited guests review the year honestly — what was delivered, what fell short, and what must change.

It’s a working session, not a ceremony. Attendance is limited to the Board and invited dignitaries so the discussion stays candid.

Areas of work

Five areas, one mission

Every program above falls under one or more of these areas.

Health education, awareness campaigns, screenings and outreach that help people seek care early and avoid preventable illness — including hygiene, nutrition, and mother-and-child health.

Medical assistance, referrals and links to hospitals for people blocked from care by cost, distance or circumstance.

Counselling, trauma-aware community support, rehabilitation and help returning to normal life after illness or crisis.

Protection and support for women, children, young people, older people and people living with disabilities — including help for struggling households.

Life-skills training, small-business support and relief in emergencies, built with local leaders so the community owns the result.

We record who we help and what changes, before and after each program, so our impact is measured — not just claimed.

How we work

Close to the community, open about results

We listen first

Every program starts with community leaders and the people affected — what they need, not what we assume.

We work with others

Hospitals, government agencies and other charities help us reach further than we could alone.

We report back

We track results and publish them, including what didn’t work, at our annual Balm Summit and online.
