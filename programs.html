Programs & thematic areas

What we actually do

Community-centred health and social intervention programs across Nigeria — five thematic areas and four flagship initiatives: Reach the Sick, OPTRTE, The Balm Basket and The Balm Summit.

Where we are today: TBHISI was incorporated in November 2025. The programs below describe our operating model and intervention areas as defined in our organizational strategy. As specific outreach events, partnerships and beneficiary numbers are delivered, we will publish real dates, locations and outcomes here rather than placeholder statistics.

01

Public Health Promotion & Preventive Healthcare

We promote preventive healthcare practices and community health awareness through health education, sensitization campaigns, screenings and outreach programs targeted at improving health-seeking behaviour and reducing preventable disease across Nigerian communities.

  • Community health awareness campaigns
  • Preventive healthcare education
  • Health outreach and screening programs
  • Hygiene and sanitation promotion
  • Nutrition and wellness education
  • Communicable and non-communicable disease awareness
  • Maternal, newborn and child health education
02

Healthcare Support & Medical Assistance

We provide support initiatives that improve access to healthcare services for vulnerable individuals and underserved communities, particularly those facing financial, social or systemic barriers to healthcare access.

  • Medical assistance and referral support
  • Healthcare access interventions
  • Support for vulnerable patients and families
  • Community-based healthcare initiatives
  • Linkages to healthcare providers and institutions
  • Health emergency response support where applicable
03

Mental Health & Psychosocial Support (MHPSS)

Recognising the growing need for mental and emotional wellbeing services in Nigeria, we implement psychosocial support interventions aimed at promoting mental health awareness, emotional resilience, rehabilitation and social reintegration.

  • Mental health awareness campaigns
  • Psychosocial support services
  • Counselling and emotional support initiatives
  • Trauma-informed community interventions
  • Rehabilitation and reintegration support
  • Community-based support networks
04

Women, Children & Vulnerable Population Support

We prioritise the protection, empowerment and wellbeing of women, children, youths, elderly persons, persons with disabilities and other vulnerable groups through inclusive social intervention programs.

  • Women and girl-child empowerment initiatives
  • Child welfare and protection programs
  • Support for persons with disabilities
  • Social protection and welfare support
  • Gender-responsive community interventions
  • Vulnerable household support services
05

Community Development & Social Intervention

We design and implement social intervention initiatives that strengthen community resilience, improve livelihoods and address social challenges affecting vulnerable populations in Nigeria.

  • Community empowerment initiatives
  • Social welfare interventions
  • Community reintegration programs
  • Capacity-building and life skills training
  • Poverty alleviation support initiatives
  • Humanitarian and relief interventions where necessary
Flagship initiatives

Our named programs

Alongside our thematic areas, we run four standing initiatives — each with its own rhythm, its own people, and its own way of reaching them.

01
Quarterly

Reach the Sick

Four times a year, our team visits people admitted in hospital — those facing long treatment, thin support networks, or a ward far from home.

We help financially where we can. But the heart of this program is emotional support: sitting with patients, listening, and reminding them they have not been forgotten. Alongside it we offer mental and psychosocial care — easing the anxiety, isolation and discouragement that illness brings, and that treatment alone does not address.

Where a patient consents, we follow up beyond the visit rather than leaving encouragement at the bedside.

02
Full-cycle intervention

OPTRTE

Operation Pick, Treat, Rehabilitate, Teach and Empower

Our most intensive program: a complete cycle that carries one person from crisis to self-sufficiency, rather than offering relief and moving on.

Beneficiaries are selected at random, not by application — so that help reaches people who would never think to ask for it, and who are usually missed by programs that require you to come forward.

  • Pick — we identify and take in an individual in urgent need
  • Treat — we provide the medical care their condition requires
  • Rehabilitate — we support their physical and psychosocial recovery
  • Teach — we equip them with a skill or trade
  • Empower — we set them up with the means to sustain themselves
03
Ongoing

The Balm Basket

Not every need announces itself as a medical emergency. The Balm Basket exists for households under quiet strain — where the pressing need is food on the table, a working home, or simply getting through the month.

We provide direct financial support alongside relief items, provisions and essential household goods. It is delivered quietly and with dignity: the point is that a family is steadier afterwards, not that the giving was seen.

04
Annual · By invitation

The Balm Summit

Held at the close of each year, the Summit brings together our Board of Trustees and invited dignitaries to review the year’s work honestly — what was delivered, what fell short, and what has to change.

It is a working session rather than a ceremony. Attendance is strictly limited to the Board and invited dignitaries, so the discussion stays candid and the decisions taken carry into the year ahead.

How we implement

Community-based, partnership-driven

Direct engagement

Community-based outreach, stakeholder consultations, needs assessments and inclusion of local leaders and community actors.

Strategic partnerships

Collaboration with healthcare institutions, government agencies, civil society organizations and development partners to strengthen reach.

Monitoring & learning

Routine field monitoring, beneficiary data collection, baseline and end-line evaluations, and continuous program improvement.
